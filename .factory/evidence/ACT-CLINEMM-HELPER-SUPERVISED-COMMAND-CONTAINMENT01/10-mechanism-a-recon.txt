CANDIDATE A — CLEANUP-TIME ANCESTRY RECONSTRUCTION
==================================================

Mechanism:
    root PID
    → enumerate process table at cleanup time
    → recursively select descendants by CURRENT ppid
    → terminate matched identities

Test driver:  tools/macos-host-helper/native/containment-probe/11-mechanism-a-helper-driven.mjs

Why helper-driven:
    The test sandbox isolates signal authority. My user cannot kill
    processes spawned by the fixture (verified: "Operation not permitted"
    for the spawned rootPid, identical to the production EPERM substrate).
    The clinemm-host-helper is a per-user LaunchAgent with EPERM authority
    to deliver signals across the sandbox boundary. The driver uses
    client.open + process-group.register-owned + process-group.terminate-owned
    to perform the inherited-PGID kill, exactly as production CommandJobManager
    does today.

Verdict:
    MECHANISM_A = REFUTED

    Cleanup-time current-ppid BFS cannot reach a descendant that has
    called setsid() (Python) or detached:true (Node). The detached
    descendant becomes the leader of its own session/PGID; its
    current ppid becomes 1 (or some other reparenting target) once
    its parent dies. macOS deliberately distinguishes current ppid
    from original_ppid; the current ppid is rewritten on reparenting.
    Apple Developer docs:
      ppid: "The parent process identifier. ..."
      "After fork(), the child PPID is the parent's PID."
    There is no "original_ppid" in the user-visible kinfo_proc / proc_info;
    restoration from a process snapshot is therefore not possible on
    macOS without kqueue-style event-time tracking.

    Helper-mediated PGID cleanup of the inherited PGID is necessary
    but not sufficient: it kills the inherited members, but the
    detached child is in a different PGID and survives.

A_CURRENT_PPID_RECONSTRUCTION = REFUTED

No false-ownership was introduced. The unrelated-control check is
deferred to a later phase; this candidate was rejected on correctness
grounds (misses the escape), not on over-reach grounds.

The non-helper-driven variant
(tools/macos-host-helper/native/containment-probe/10-mechanism-a-cleanup-ancestry.mjs)
was attempted first and surfaced the substrate signal-authority
boundary: my driver cannot kill the spawned fixture's rootPid
(verified directly with `kill -KILL rootPid`: "Operation not permitted").
This is the SAME EPERM substrate the production CommandJobManager
sees, and is the reason the helper exists. The helper-driven variant
exercises the production cleanup authority and reaches the same
conclusion: A is REFUTED for the E/F escapes.
