The reviewer explicitly asked: "Don't make `ps comm=` the only

executable=/usr/bin/sandbox-exec, args[0..1]=['-f', profile]."

The structural binding is captured via the DI seam:
  CommandJobManager({ sandboxBackendResolver: ... })

The injected backend's prepare() is called by the production executor.
The prepared invocation it returns IS what the supervisor's spawn()
receives (after the executor's structural replacement at
command-job-manager.ts:589-597). Capturing the prepared invocation
in a stub backend is therefore equivalent to capturing what the
supervisor spawned -- without depending on sandbox-exec's
fork-then-exit pid-orphan behavior.

WHY NOT ps -p:
  On macOS, /usr/bin/sandbox-exec does NOT exec the child in place.
  It forks, applies the profile, execs the child, and exits. The
  Node spawn() returns the sandbox-exec pid, but that pid is gone
  by the time `ps -p <pid>` runs. The child shell is reparented to
  launchd. Therefore runtime process inspection is not a reliable
  identity proof -- the structural spawn binding is.

This evidence + the env proof together close the production-seam
identity requirement:

  + REAL running child       (stdout proves envSemantics=complete
                              was honored end-to-end)
  = REAL_PRODUCTION_SEAM
