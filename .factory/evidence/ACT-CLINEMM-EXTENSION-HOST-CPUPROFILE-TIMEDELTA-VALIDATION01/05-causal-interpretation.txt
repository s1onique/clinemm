ACT-CLINEMM-EXTENSION-HOST-CPUPROFILE-TIMEDELTA-VALIDATION01 — causal interpretation
====================================================================================

Empirical facts (proven by the raw cpuprofile data):
----------------------------------------------------
F1. idx=0 is the FIRST sample of the profile.
F2. samples[0] = 15 = node id of cwi.
F3. timeDeltas[0] = 2,189,353 us = 2189.353 ms.
F4. There is no sample at samples[-1] to attribute this interval to.
F5. By the "timeDelta attributed to top-of-stack leaf" convention,
    the entire 2189.353 ms interval is credited to cwi at idx=0.
F6. The next 11 cwi samples are at indices 972, 1867, 5651, 7538, 9196,
    18840, 24510, 24609, 27878, 37217, 37993.
F7. Each of those 11 samples has timeDelta 128-130 us. None exceed 130 us.
F8. The maximum single-delta for ANY control leaf (GC, Rnl, drain, r_)
    is at most 1898 us = 1.898 ms.
F9. 2189.353 ms (cwi idx=0) is 1154x larger than the max control delta.

Why F1-F9 cannot mean "cwi burned 2189 ms":
--------------------------------------------
   cwi is inlined at the hot-path entry of handleSessionEvent.
   Its body is THREE integer mutations:
      _nestedDepth++
      _counters.handleSessionEventCalls++
      _counters.maxNestedHandleDepth = _nestedDepth
   The body executes in microseconds. It cannot be on top of stack
   for 2.2 seconds of CPU; the V8 sampling clock would have
   interrupted it long before that.

Three (and only three) explanations are consistent with F1-F9:
--------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Capture-start lag.
    The cpuprofile writer started recording 2.2 s AFTER cwi's call first
    reached the top of stack. This is consistent with the profiler
    attaching mid-extension-activation, and explains the entire 30.45%
    attribution as a writer artifact.
(2) Profile-writer gap.
    The cpuprofile writer itself froze the event loop for 2.2 s while
    writing the profile metadata, with cwi still on top of stack from
    the dispatch that triggered the freeze (e.g. a session event
    arriving during write serialization). CPU during that 2.2 s is
    NOT attributed to cwi; it is just unattributed or attributed to
    whatever native code the writer invoked.
(3) Sampling suspension.
    V8's sampling thread was briefly suspended (e.g. GC initialization,
    inspector attach, etc.) for 2.2 s. cwi remained on top of stack
    throughout. CPU during the suspension is NOT attributed to cwi.

In ALL THREE cases, the ~2189 ms is NOT cwi's CPU cost. The "30.45% of
profile" attribution to cwi is an artifact of the inter-sample gap
landing adjacent to (specifically, on) a cwi stack frame at idx=0.
