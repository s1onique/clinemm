FOREIGN PROCESS CONSERVATION (R5)

The repair MUST never degrade into:
  pkill <command_name>
  kill 0
  killall
  ps | grep | awk | xargs kill

Conservation invariant:
  HOST_SPAWNED(P_own) AND FOREIGN_PID != P_own.pgid
  ⇒ FOREIGN_PID.alive = true throughout termination of P_own.

POSIX guarantee (the substrate the primitive relies on):
  process.kill(-pgid, signal) signals ONLY processes whose PGID == pgid.
  A foreign process in a DIFFERENT process group is NEVER touched.

Why PGIDs provide the isolation:
  - each spawn with detached:true creates a NEW process group (pgid == pid)
  - the foreign process is a child of some other parent, has its OWN pgid
  - even if the foreign process happens to be a child of OUR parent (the
    Node host), its pgid is distinct from ours (because we are detached)
  - signalGroup(pgid) sends to ALL processes whose pgid == pgid
  - the foreign process's pgid != pgid -> not signaled

The terminateTree primitive does NOT use:
  - ps + grep + awk
  - pgrep
  - command-name matching
  - pid-table scanning

It uses ONLY:
  - process.kill(-pgid, signal) — group-targeted
  - process.kill(-pgid, 0) — group existence probe

The same group identity is used for both signalling and probing, so the
foreign process is structurally inaccessible to the primitive.

VERIFIED — the production repair (buildShellProcess / terminateTree) uses
ONLY the PGID acquired at spawn time and never scans the pid table or
matches by command name.

Fail-safe: even in the substrate-blocked authoring shell, the foreign
process conservation holds because process.kill(-pgid, signal) returns
EPERM when the call cannot complete, NOT because it accidentally
signalled a different process. The primitive cannot reach foreign
processes regardless of substrate state.
