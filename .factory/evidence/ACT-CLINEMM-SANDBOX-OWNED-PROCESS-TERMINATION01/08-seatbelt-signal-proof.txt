SEATBELT SIGNAL POLICY ANALYSIS

Current Cline Seatbelt profile (sdk/packages/core/src/runtime/sandbox/macos/seatbelt-profile.ts:10):
  (allow signal (target self))

This is the MINIMAL Seatbelt signal grant. It permits the sandboxed
process to signal itself (e.g. for setpgid, self-management), and
denies everything else by default.

Two distinct questions must be separated:
  Q1. Does the Cline Seatbelt profile block the SANDBOXED CHILD from
      receiving signals from its parent?
  Q2. Does the Cline Seatbelt profile block the SANDBOXED CHILD from
      sending signals to other sandboxed children in the same profile?

Q1 ANSWER (load-bearing for this ACT):
  No. Seatbelt enforces OUTGOING syscalls from the sandboxed process.
  Signals arriving at the sandboxed process from outside are
  kernel-routed normally. The parent's `process.kill(-pgid, signal)`
  reaches the sandboxed child through the kernel's standard signal
  delivery path; the child's Seatbelt profile does not interpose on
  signal RECEIPT.

  Empirical confirmation: the previous ACT-CLINEMM-SEATBELT-NETWORK-
  EGRESS-RECON01 substrate tests succeeded under the production
  Seatbelt profile (with `(allow signal (target self))`) and proved
  that signal delivery from parent to sandboxed child works.

Q2 ANSWER (NOT load-bearing for this ACT):
  Out of scope. The supervisor does not require the sandboxed child
  to signal its peers. The sandboxed child is a LEAF process in our
  model; all coordination happens via the host Node parent, which is
  NOT inside the Cline Seatbelt sandbox.

VERDICT — current Seatbelt profile IS sufficient.

The supervisor's signal primitive operates from the HOST (outside the
Cline Seatbelt sandbox). The host Node process is not confined by the
child's Seatbelt profile; it sends signals directly via `kill(2)`.

The host Node process IS confined by its OWN sandbox (in the IDE-
sandboxed authoring shell: VSCodium Helper Chromium zygote's
`--enable-sandbox`). That is a DIFFERENT sandbox and does NOT concern
Cline's Seatbelt policy.

NO SEATBELT POLICY CHANGE REQUIRED for this ACT.
